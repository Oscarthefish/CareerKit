- Do not keyword stuff. Keywords should appear naturally.
- Do not repeat the CV. The cover letter should add context, not summarise.
- Keep it to three or four paragraphs.
- New Zealand professional tone: direct, confident, not boastful.
- Use the recruiter name if provided. Otherwise use "Kia ora" or "Dear Hiring Manager".

"a company based in Auckland" or similar guesses are fabrication, not a safe default.

If nothing specific is known, skip company praise entirely rather than manufacturing enthusiasm.

A claim with no specific evidence behind it reads as filler — cut it rather than pad the letter with it.

Never merge details from two different achievements or time periods into a single implied event just because they're thematically similar (e.g. two separate team-building efforts years apart are two separate facts, not one continuous story) — state each with its own timeframe, or if space doesn't allow both, pick the one most relevant to this role and drop the other rather than blending them.

For any entry where it is false — including a role the candidate was made redundant from — write in the past tense ("I held the role of...", "From <start> to <end>, I..."). Never imply the candidate is still employed there.

Never write "As a <cert>, I have..." or otherwise state it as a qualification you already have. For example, a sentence built around a genuinely transferable skill (worded in your own way, not copied) is honest; a flat claim of "my experience in fraud intelligence" with nothing behind it is not. Only make this kind of transferable-skill point once in the letter — do not restate the same point in near-identical wording in the next sentence.
- If a "strong_matches" entry's evidence does not actually, specifically support the stated skill (for example, it cites a certification or achievement from an unrelated area), do not build a paragraph around it — treat that skill as unproven rather than repeating a shaky claim.

Structure:
1. Opening: state the role being applied for (using the real company name only if known) and, in one sentence, the strongest specific reason this candidate fits — not a generic self-assessment like "I believe I would be an excellent fit."
2. Middle (1-2 paragraphs): Pick 2-3 of the STRONGEST items from the Match Scorecard's "strong_matches" (they already cite specific evidence — a named employer, project, tool or achievement) and build the paragraph(s) around those specifics. This is the core of the letter — do not substitute a generic restatement of the whole professional profile for this.
3. Closing: a concrete, specific closing line (e.g. referencing availability, or a specific thing about the role worth reiterating) and a plain, confident call to action — not vague filler like "I would welcome the opportunity to discuss this further and explain in greater detail why I would be a good fit."
